Earlier research and design work, from graduate school at Carnegie Mellon and a product design consultancy. Each one shaped how I design AI products today.

## IoT Hub: security people don't have to think about

![](thumb-iot-hub.webp)

Carnegie Mellon HCII research · 2017 · Researcher, designer, lead author

People don't go to their devices to "do security," so we designed a home hub that keeps them safe by default. Each device declares the few places it's allowed to connect, and the hub blocks everything else and watches for software updates. The paper also weighs how much to automate against how much control people give up, the same question I now ask of AI agents.

[Read the paper](assets/files/iot-hub-paper.pdf)

## Knowledge Accelerator for Programmers

![](thumb-knowledge-accelerator.webp)

Carnegie Mellon HCII research · 2017 · Graduate research assistant

Walkthroughs with 14 programmers about a hard task they had just finished: where they searched, how they judged a source, and when they finally asked someone for help. Almost nobody took notes, so any tool that captures what people learn has to pay off for them right away, not just for the next person.

## Twenty20: retail buying software

![](thumb-twenty20.webp)

LH Ventures and Carnegie Mellon · 2017 · UX research lead

We talked with more than 60 retail professionals and found that great buyers think in stories, not spreadsheets. That became the design principle "bring forward the story, not the data," and led to a visual tool for exploring past and present buys, with saved views, alerts and notes. We tested it as a working web prototype.

## Portable ECMO interface

![](thumb-ecmo.webp)

Medical device client, via a design consultancy · 2018 · Interaction designer

The touchscreen for a portable heart-lung machine used in ambulances and emergency rooms. When a mistake can cost a life, every alarm, value and control has to be unambiguous at a glance. I mapped its patterns to the monitors clinicians already know, wireframed the core workflows, and kept the style guide that developers built from.
